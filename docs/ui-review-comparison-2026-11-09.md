## What the models added beyond the handwritten notes

These are **additional candidates**, not things you necessarily failed to notice, and not automatically true. Your sheet is not an exhaustive user acceptance test.

- **Cross-view identity and navigation:** avatars with/without initials; inconsistent megaphone/lock/hash channel markers; page/subtitle duplication; differing reading edges and form widths; header/icon-axis alignment.
- **Overview information:** duplicate Members/Member and online labels; decorative banner; search in an empty announcements panel; unread-empty noise; the club's state being secondary. “Missing active/inactive status” follows a demand in the brief and is not yet an owner-confirmed feature requirement.
- **Permissions:** bare permission counts that look like headcounts; collapsed groups hiding enabled-state summaries; empty checkboxes meaning any role; uncertain meaning of exceptions; no posting-rights summary in the channel list; the read-only description contradicting the displayed posting value.
- **Detailed rendering:** helper placement, dropdown chevron placement, off-toggle styling, split-panel dividers, chat/composer insets, settings-row action axes, and nested card/list edges.
- **Candidates needing particular skepticism:** all icon-only buttons need visible labels; every repeated title is useless; all empty states need identical layouts; widths must be equal across unlike pages; every destructive action needs the same styling. These are not established requirements just because a brief made the model look for consistency.

There are concrete false or overreaching claims. Haiku B3 F27 says Settings is unselected on Channels; the capture visibly highlights Settings. Several Luna outputs say the setup pill is absent on capture 3; it is visible there. Haiku B2 F13 turns ambiguous permission counts into a claim of wrong role data, although the detail subtitle points to permission counts. Haiku A H2 F21 treats “everyone in the club”, “everyone with access”, roles and units as interchangeable; those represent different rules. The pill's presence is intended, so its existence alone is not a valid defect.

## What this changes about the skill's purpose

The aim should be to **reduce the owner's effort needed to reach a coherent, task-appropriate interface**, while preserving product meaning. Cheap screenshot inference is one candidate technique. It has to earn its place against a simpler direct review.

A useful decomposition is by the evidence required, rather than fixed model tiers:

| Judgment | Minimum useful context | What still needs investigation |
|---|---|---|
| Visual relation: edges, contrast, density, type roles, control treatment | Full captures plus intended design conventions | Whether variants serve different jobs; contrast measurements if making numeric claims |
| Task fit: redundant text, prominence, secondary controls | Actor, actual goal, frequency, experience and intended behavior | Whether the removed text/control carries information for a less experienced person |
| Product structure: units vs channels, permission logic, Chat/Whiteboards relation | A short domain contract and a representative workflow | Correct abstraction, existing behavior, constraints and deliberate changes to them |
| Implementation | Agreed problem/desired behavior, source and callsites | Primitive versus misuse, permission effects, regressions |

One capable reviewer may do all four on a small app. A large repeated review may amortize preparation and benefit from cheaper independent finders. No evidence here requires a large orchestrator specifically, nor demonstrates that a medium fixer can safely repair every product-structure issue. Role labels should describe the work and evidence; choose the model after measuring it.

The app's [current documented contract](/Users/sreysus/workflow/ia-app/docs/features/clubs.md:242) is already enough to explain much of the unit/channel problem without giving every finder the codebase:

- A **unit** is a named organizational subgroup, potentially nested (team, committee, room, etc.). People may belong to multiple units.
- A **channel** is a communication space. Its role filter, unit-based audience and sidebar placement are independent.
- The role filter is OR within roles; roles AND unit filtering combine; explicit people are additional OR exceptions; some managers bypass restrictions.
- There can be automatic private channels for new units and a separate backfill action for existing units.

Those are facts from the current documentation, not a fresh verification of the captured revision's backend. They show why both “just explain it” and “just remove the subtitle” can miss the design task: what is the smallest user-facing set of concepts that still expresses the intended audience and organization? “Indent” might sound cosmetic while access is configured separately. The interface needs to distinguish placement from access, especially if the owner expects them to be coupled.

Give a finder only the facts its judgment needs. Visual comparison may need none of this domain detail. A product review needs it, plus a concrete example such as an existing club team and its conversation. The orchestrator/source reader can establish these facts once; it need not ask every cheap finder to rediscover them. Document which facts are intended behavior and which are only observed behavior, so the implementation does not become an unquestionable design requirement.

Your System One/System Two reflection fits as a practical intuition: familiarity may make a complex relationship feel immediate to you. That does not make it obvious to a new user, and a model's extra reasoning tokens do not prove it is doing the same comparison. This evaluation changes models, providers, prompting and reasoning amounts; it does not isolate the cause of Haiku's better output. The actionable distinction is how much context the judgment requires, not whether it is intrinsically “fast” or “slow”.

## The next experiment that would justify the architecture

This is a proposed next experiment, **not an additional batch launched here**. Compare (1) one capable reviewer directly reviewing and implementing, (2) one capable review followed by a fixer, and (3) the orchestrated cheap-finder workflow. Give each the same captures, domain contract, task, design intent and access to necessary evidence. Track total owner labeling time, accepted unique findings, important omissions, bad proposed changes, elapsed time, attributable usage, and the quality of the implemented result. Preparation, merging, exclusions and rework all count.

Use untouched natural pages for evaluation. The bottom-gap example is now trained against in the ordinary experimental sense: we revised the brief knowing it. Include mature/intentional UI in the sample to expose false positives; do not force every finder to emit a list of defects.
